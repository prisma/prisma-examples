Blue hour is the stretch after sunset — and before sunrise — when the sun sits between roughly 4 and 8 degrees below the horizon. The sky holds a deep, even blue, and its brightness briefly matches the brightness of streetlights and windows.

## Why the balance matters

Earlier in the evening, the sky overpowers artificial light; later, windows and lamps blow out against a black sky. During blue hour both sit within the same exposure range, which is why city scenes shot in this window look luminous without any blending or compositing.

## Timing

Despite the name, the usable window is often 20 to 40 minutes depending on latitude and season. Sun-position apps report civil and nautical twilight precisely; the strongest color usually falls in the second half of civil twilight.

## Working method

Exposures run long — typically between half a second and 15 seconds at low ISO — so a tripod is effectively required. Arrive at the location before sunset, compose in daylight, and then shoot the same frame repeatedly as the light falls. The differences between frames five minutes apart are larger than they seem in the moment.
