Fog is one of the most forgiving and most deceptive conditions to shoot in. The light is soft and even, but almost everything the camera does automatically works against you.

## Exposure

Meters read fog as too bright and underexpose it into grey. Adding between one and two stops of exposure compensation usually restores the scene; check the histogram rather than the preview, since the preview is unreliable in flat light.

## Focus

Autofocus systems hunt in fog because there is little contrast to lock onto. Manual focus on a defined edge — a branch, a fence line, a roofline — is faster and more reliable. Depth of field matters less than usual, as the fog itself limits how much detail exists at distance.

## Timing

Fog moves more quickly than it appears to. A composition that works can dissolve in under a minute, and the best separation between layers often happens as the fog begins to lift rather than at its thickest. Arriving before first light and staying an hour past sunrise covers most of the useful window.
