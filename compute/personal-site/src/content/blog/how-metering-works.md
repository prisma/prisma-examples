title: How camera metering actually works
description: Every camera meter assumes the world is middle grey. Understanding that single assumption explains most exposure surprises.
publishedAt: 2026-03-04
---

A camera's light meter does not measure how bright a scene is. It measures how much light reflects off the scene and assumes the answer should average out to middle grey — about 18% reflectance.

## Why this matters

Snow, sand, and white walls reflect far more than 18%, so the meter darkens them toward grey. Dark clothing, shadowed forests, and night scenes reflect less, so the meter brightens them. In both cases the meter is behaving correctly by its own rules and incorrectly for the picture.

## The metering modes

Evaluative or matrix metering divides the frame into zones and weights them against a database of scene patterns. Center-weighted metering averages the frame with emphasis on the middle. Spot metering reads a small area — typically 2 to 5% of the frame — and ignores everything else.

## A practical approach

Spot-meter something in the scene whose brightness you know: green grass sits close to middle grey, and the palm of your hand holds a constant offset from it — meter your palm against a grey card once and you can carry that reference anywhere. It is slower than trusting the matrix, and more consistent.
